# The Captain's Quiet Match — A2

História original assistida por IA para English Roulette. Uma capitã aprende que ajudar a equipe pode significar falar menos e abrir espaço para a voz de uma nova jogadora.

**Gênero:** Esporte · **Nível proposto:** A2. Cinco cenas, quatro por percurso e dois finais completos. Nível editorial, sem certificação CEFR ou declaração de revisão humana.

## The Loudest Voice (`too-many-words`)

Mika was captain of a small indoor football team. Before the evening match, she called instructions across the court. June, a new player, looked at her and then at the ball. "I can hear you, but there are too many words." Mika stopped. She wanted to help her team, but speaking louder was not making her message easier to understand.

**Pergunta:** What problem does June describe?

- **a** — The court has no lights
- **b** — There are too many words in the instructions
- **c** — The ball is missing

**Gabarito editorial:** b. There are too many words in the instructions. Evidence: there are too many words

**Dicas:** court → quadra; instructions → instruções.

## One Idea at a Time (`one-arrow`)

The coach called a short break before the match began. Mika sat beside June on the bench and asked what would help. "One idea at a time. Tell me where you need me." Mika drew a simple arrow on a small whiteboard. June pointed to the open space beside the net and nodded. The picture was easier to follow than a long speech.

**Pergunta:** What makes the plan easier to follow?

- **a** — A louder speech across the court
- **b** — A long list of new rules
- **c** — A simple arrow on a whiteboard

**Gabarito editorial:** c. A simple arrow on a whiteboard. Evidence: The picture was easier to follow than a long speech.

**Dicas:** bench → banco de reservas; nodded → concordou com a cabeça.

## More than One Useful Voice (`captains-choice`)

Mika had two ways to share the simpler plan. She could stay on the court, use short calls and point to open spaces. She could also begin on the bench and let June explain the arrow to the team. "Being captain means helping everyone understand. It does not mean saying every word myself." June waited for Mika's choice.

**Pergunta:** What can June do if Mika starts on the bench?

- **a** — Explain the arrow to the team
- **b** — Cancel the match
- **c** — Take the ball home

**Gabarito editorial:** a. Explain the arrow to the team. Evidence: let June explain the arrow to the team

**Dicas:** point → apontar; choice → escolha.

## A Pass They Both Understood (`short-calls`)

Mika stayed on the court. Instead of a long speech, she used June's name and one clear instruction. June moved into the open space and received a pass. "That was it. We understood each other." The next move did not end in a goal, but their team kept the ball. Mika saw June ask for it again. They had found a way to work together.

**Pergunta:** What happens after June receives a pass?

- **a** — The match is cancelled immediately
- **b** — The team keeps the ball without scoring a goal
- **c** — June leaves the team

**Gabarito editorial:** b. The team keeps the ball without scoring a goal. Evidence: The next move did not end in a goal, but their team kept the ball.

**Dicas:** pass → passe; kept → manteve.

Fim deste percurso.

## The Plan in Her Own Words (`junes-plan`)

Mika began on the bench and handed June the whiteboard. June explained the arrow in her own words. She asked one teammate to show the movement, then checked that the others understood. "Captain, did I miss anything?" "No. You made the plan yours." When Mika joined the court later, she listened before speaking. Her team now had more than one useful voice.

**Pergunta:** What does Mika do when she joins the court later?

- **a** — She removes every player from the court
- **b** — She refuses to hear June
- **c** — She listens before speaking

**Gabarito editorial:** c. She listens before speaking. Evidence: she listened before speaking

**Dicas:** handed → entregou; teammate → colega de equipe.

Fim deste percurso.
